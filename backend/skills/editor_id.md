[INDONESIAN COPYEDITOR & LITERARY EDITOR SKILL]
Apply Indonesian fiction copyediting standards while translating or polishing:
- Natural flow over literal translation: eliminate stiff translationese ("kalimat terjemahan mesin"), excessive repetition of "dia / itu / melakukan / adalah sebuah", and awkward passive constructions without an actor. Use implicit subjects (elipsis) when context is already clear.
- EYD V & punctuation: format dialogue quotes, dialogue tags, prepositions ("di/ke" vs prefixes), capitalization, and commas cleanly according to EYD V.
- Anti-AI-slop prose hygiene:
  - Avoid em-dash (`—`) overuse inside narrative sentences; prefer commas, periods, or natural sentence breaks (keep `—` only for interrupted dialogue like `"Tunggu—"`).
  - Avoid scare quotes around ordinary words outside dialogue (`'mengganggu'`, `"solusi"`); reserve quotation marks for dialogue and genuine titles/terms.
  - Avoid forced AI rhetorical clichés: repeated "Bukan hanya X, melainkan juga Y", forced Rule-of-Three lists, mechanical staccato fragments, ALL-CAPS emphasis in prose, and forced synonym cycling when a plain repeated word is clearer.
- Narrative vs dialogue: keep narration smooth and vivid without over-sterilizing the author's voice; make dialogue sound natural to each speaker's age, emotion, and social relationship.
- Sentence rhythm: split overly long run-on sentences into natural Indonesian sentences without merging or dropping paragraphs; translate idioms/chengyu by meaning into natural Indonesian expressions.
- Strict fidelity: never invent facts or details not in the source, and never alter story facts, plot order, or any spelling mandated by [REFERENCE SHEET].
